Chainy design brief​

Capstone project title: Chainy: A 3D World Educational Game for learning emerging Technology (Blockchain & AI)

Approved scope supersession — October 8, 2026: The user selected essential fundamentals for ages 12–17, especially underserved/remote learners with limited technology access, through tappable Web3, Blockchain and AI worlds. Full A–Z coverage is withdrawn. Free spatial construction and mandatory walkable 3D/joystick navigation are deferred; use bounded order/link activities, guided three-local-replica comparison, a genuine tiny classifier validated before production, and a short fixed-grid agent. The nine lesson IDs W1–W3, B1–B3 and A1–A3 are a draft curriculum, not a confirmed count. Current baseline/GDD/curriculum take precedence over historical issue titles and old level IDs below. Phone specifications, SQLite/export, rubric and November 21 deadline remain. The old 520-hour estimate has not been recosted for this scope.

Version 0.2 · October 8, 2026 · Confirmed visual direction; production details proposed for team review.

This brief translates the current decisions and game design document into a small art, interface, and content production package. It specifies intended work, not completed assets or measured usability. Game assets and Unity prefabs belong in Chainy-Game; this repository holds their requirements and review records.

Direction and decision status​

Confirmed: English first; ages 12–17; offline single-player Android; a tappable world-and-lesson map; short lessons across Web3, Blockchain and AI; bounded record-ordering and linking activities; blockchain synchronization, example-trained classification, and an agent challenge; badges and level progress as initial rewards. Touchscreen controls and Unity/C# are the technical direction.

Confirmed visual direction (October 8): the user selected the first two Chainy storyboards as the definite style: cute, rounded toy-like 3D; the blue-haired explorer in yellow; friendly screen-faced robots; cubic island scenery; bright blue/green Blockchain World and violet AI World. Preserve these silhouettes, proportions, and friendly presentation. The 12–17 audience informs the reasoning, instructional copy, and challenge depth; it does not authorize replacing this art with realistic teenage characters. Reference sheet 1 and reference sheet 2 are the visual authority. Exact palette tokens, font licensing, model sources and final production assets still need specification.

Gameplay reference only: the third BlockForge image contributes records, linked blocks, ordering, tamper detection, verification, quizzes and node synchronization. Its flat drawing style, title, coins and certificate are not adopted design requirements. Storyboard counters do not confirm lesson counts. Bounded order/link choices are now approved; free spatial construction is deferred.

Recommended production approach: translate the confirmed style into a small reusable mobile asset kit, short practical missions and readable diagrams. Use tappable world/lesson cards, reusable explanation/activity panels, bounded order/link choices, guided replica displays and a short fixed-grid agent. The classifier needs a genuine learning prototype first. No walkable scene or joystick is required; toy-like 3D art remains approved. The GDD media register distinguishes supplied references, generated concepts and runtime-ready assets.

The pre-final defense remains November 21, 2026. Prioritize comprehensible learning interactions over scenery quantity. CH-10 targets the reusable kit on October 25. The possible November 3–6 beta window at Tambunan National High School, Marinduque requires an integrated and checked build beforehand; follow the reconciled schedule in the planning documents. Preserve November integration, QA, pilot work, and rehearsal. Visual readiness cannot establish the school's undefined 60-percent completion requirement.

Visual and instructional hierarchy​

A player should identify the objective, the next available action, and the result of the last action without depending on narration. Use practical English and consistent words: record, link, validate, example, prediction, and policy. Introduce each term with the activity that demonstrates it.

Recommended visual hierarchy:

  1. The active objective and selected object stand out from decorative scenery.
  2. Components show a stable icon and short label; selected components reveal their configuration and ports.
  3. Validation marks the relevant object or connection and provides a short explanation plus a repair action.
  4. Rewards confirm progress without implying certified mastery or interrupting correction.

Use color together with shape, icons, labels, and connection patterns. A red line alone is insufficient to explain an invalid connection. Distinguish local replica agreement from internet connectivity; neither Wi-Fi imagery nor an online status indicator should imply a real network. Show classifier distance as distance, never as a confidence percentage. Label the agent's rule sequence and execution trace so it is not mistaken for generative AI.

Proposed model convention: 1 Unity unit represents 1 meter, environment pieces share an agreed grid, and placeable objects use predictable ground pivots. Confirm the first sample with the technical lead before bulk export. Use names such as CH_Block_Record, CH_Node_Replica, CH_UI_QuizPanel, and CH_SFX_Validate; distinguish source and runtime derivatives in the register.

Source art belongs in a separate editable-source folder in the game repository; Unity-ready assets go under Assets/Chainy/Art and Assets/Chainy/Audio, with UI prefabs under the agreed UI area. Commit Unity metadata with imported assets. Keep large unsupported source formats out of runtime resource folders. Third-party work must retain attribution/license text; generated assets still require provenance and visual review.

Proposed resource and import constraints​

Use the hardware validation plan as the shared budget owner. Its 30 FPS, p95 frame time at most 40 ms, process memory at most 800 MiB, and APK at most 500 MB are proposed targets, not measured results. The candidate device class is 4 GB physical RAM; Android 11+ is provisional, with Android 10 subject to testing. No device list or performance certification is available.

Begin with simple shared materials, modest textures, inexpensive lighting, limited transparent effects, and simple collision. Proposed starting texture cap: 1024 pixels on the longest edge for ordinary assets, with smaller icons/props and documented exceptions for readability. This is an asset-production starting point, not a proven memory allocation. Choose compression with the pinned engine and tested devices; do not assume ASTC is universally available. Record texture dimensions, compression, mipmap choice, mesh complexity, material count, and audio import settings in the sample review before producing the full kit.

Bundle all required play content and audio. Show no first-run asset download or network dependency. Reduce props, texture sizes, particles, and shadow cost before weakening required learning interactions. Measure the combined scene on a phone; individual low-poly assets do not prove the whole scene meets its budget.

Acceptance and unresolved inputs​

Review CH-R01, CH-R02, CH-R05–10 using a real target build: offline start; readable English instructions; reachable touch controls; clear bounded order/link editing; understandable agreement, classifier, and agent states; safe profile switching; persistence failures that preserve answers; and retryable export. Check enlarged text, device safe areas, long labels, sound disabled, invalid configurations, and interruption/relaunch. Visual sign-off requires the art/UI reviewers; educational clarity requires review against the GDD and subsequent actual learner evidence.

Still needed: representative phone models and resolutions within the retained specifications; confirmation of landscape orientation; production palette tokens and a licensed font; availability of editable licensed source assets/audio; approved component/content caps; per-person availability; and actual pilot feedback. The supplied Chainy style is confirmed. Runtime assets remain placeholders or reviewed kit derivatives until production and device validation are complete. These gaps do not justify inventing user-tested accessibility, measured performance, or approved research instruments.

Project logo​

Use the Chainy wordmark and compact icon for documentation branding. The gold rounded 3D lettering, star, blue linked cubes and violet AI accent follow the selected storyboard references. Preserve the exact full capstone title as selectable text on formal covers.
