Chainy project handbook​

Historical November 9 orientation. Use the current start page, lesson-collection GDD, and readiness plan for the new direction. The economy, restoration, and actual-model requirements in this handbook are superseded. Current work is documentation/setup only, pending final approval to develop.

Prepared November 9, 2026 from the current project documents and a static inspection of the local Unity checkout. This is a single-file orientation guide for understanding the project, its intended gameplay, development setup, and delivery obligations. It summarizes existing decisions without replacing the baseline, canonical game design, or team board.

The pre-final date is November 21, 2026. The November 10 Cryptita Plays community session is a gameplay beta only if the November 9 build gate passes. Otherwise, the recorded fallback is a clearly labeled concept/prototype feedback session. This handbook does not establish the outcome of that gate.

The story follows the Explorer as island services stop working. Sean guides the Web3 harbor, Matthew guides the Blockchain beacon, and Lee guides the AI assistance center. Restoring the workshop, beacon, and supervised helper gives each subject a practical purpose. The same fictional lantern purchase connects the first two worlds and provides context for later AI judgment.

The game does not require cryptocurrency, real payments, a real wallet, a public blockchain, or cloud AI. Multiplayer, combat, speculative trading, live generative chat, and a procedural open world are outside the adopted version.

Source basis is the project baseline and canonical GDD.

Audience and learning purpose​

Learners are not expected to have prior cryptocurrency or programming experience. The design introduces an example, lets the player act, explains the result, and then checks understanding in another situation. Short quizzes support the action instead of replacing the adventure with definitions.

User or stakeholderWhat they need from Chainy
LearnerUnderstandable objectives, safe fictional examples, readable controls, useful feedback, and dependable offline saves
Session facilitatorA tested build, consistent instructions, disclosed limitations, and an approved evidence procedure
Teacher in the later productEnrollment, protected access, learner-record collection, and a separate upload workflow
Development teamClear requirements, one owner per card, explicit handoffs, and reproducible build evidence
Adviser or defense panelA working demonstrated scope, accurate manuscript, actual evaluation evidence, and honest limitations

The full school model anticipates a personal phone for each learner. The beta uses a simple local demo profile. A learner code identifies a local profile; it does not authenticate a person or recover another phone's records. The five team-board accounts belong to the project team and are unrelated to learner profiles.

The intended learning outcomes are distinct. Web3 activities teach access, ownership, authorization, payment, and fees. Blockchain activities teach linked records, verification, and the difference between a stale copy and an altered copy. AI activities teach examples, predictions, testing, limitations, and human review.

Completing a quest, answering after a hint, or filling an encyclopedia entry is not proof of mastery. Independent assessment and repeated practice must remain distinguishable. The learning foundations and evaluation plan explain these boundaries.

How to play the designed core chapter​

This walkthrough describes the approved behavior. Use it as a player explanation and demonstration checklist once a named build implements the chapter. Screen names, button positions, and save feedback still need comparison with the tested build.

Start and controls​

  1. Open the installed game and select the correct local demo profile.
  2. Choose Play and enter the available world.
  3. Follow the current objective to the visible guide or learning station.
  4. Read the short explanation and perform the requested action.
  5. Check the result and wait for confirmed save feedback.
ControlIntended action
Left thumb joystickMove through the world
Right contextual buttonInteract with a nearby target
Dialogue choicesAnswer or choose while world movement is disabled
Quest logRead the current objective, location, and prerequisites
EncyclopediaRevisit a concept and its example
Select a card, then a destinationPlace or compare a record without requiring dragging
Back or pauseClose the current layer or pause without losing a committed action

The intended camera stays fixed. Jumping, combat, free-camera rotation, and precision platforming are not required. Landscape orientation is the proposed target, not a device-test result.

Web3 harbor walkthrough​

QuestWhat you doExpected result
W01, arrive and meet SeanFind Sean, inspect the quest log and encyclopedia, and view the fictional wallet and receiving addressUnderstand the next objective and distinguish shareable information from secrets
W02, help the residentCollect the repair component and return itReceive 12 fictional Chain Coins exactly once
W03, buy the lanternInspect Lantern #014 and review its 8-coin price plus a 1-coin feeConfirm a total of 9 and a remaining balance of 3

The purchase review shows the recipient, item, price, fee, and total before authorization. Canceling before submission costs nothing. Repeated taps must not purchase the same lantern twice. Equipping an owned lantern, walking, reading, and viewing balances are free. The 12-coin reward is a game allocation, not a claim that real cryptocurrency comes from errands.

The wallet displays account information and provides a fictional authorization control. The ownership record belongs to the simulated ledger. No real seed phrase, private key, payment information, or financial account belongs in this activity.

Use the references between blocks rather than their colors or timestamps to determine the order. The verified source remains read-only. The activity repairs a damaged local copy; it does not let the learner rewrite accepted history. The simulated nodes are records on one phone, not a network of learner phones. This repair activity is separate from the deferred BlockMatch mini-game.

Mistakes, saving, and progression​

A wrong answer explains the error and permits another attempt without taking essential resources. An early quest pickup remains recorded. A repeated report must not repeat a reward. Insufficient funds must show what is missing and a valid recovery route.

If saving fails, use the offered retry instead of repeating a purchase to force progress. The saved balance, inventory, ledger, quests, and world restoration must agree after relaunch. Checkpoint recovery need not restore the exact animation frame.

The full progression is Web3, then Blockchain, then AI. Completed worlds remain revisitable, and optional discoveries do not require full collection before progression. The smaller versioned demo quest list must report "intro chapter complete" rather than completion of the entire full game. An AI chapter is available only if its conditional scope is actually delivered.

The learner guide, app flow, and GDD supply the underlying rules.

Visuals, interface, and accessibility​

The approved visual identity uses a blue-haired, yellow-clad Explorer and screen-faced robot guides. The three guides reuse the robot base. Compact low-poly islands and short routes keep the next objective visible. Landmarks, signs, and quest markers work together.

Dialogue appears over the live world, with text at the bottom, choices in the middle, and portraits on the sides. World input stops while dialogue or another modal activity is active. Purchase, save, loading, and error states need distinct feedback.

The quest log separates completed, current, locked, and optional objectives. The encyclopedia records Discovered, Explained, Practiced, and Demonstrated in a new situation. These labels describe evidence in the game; none means mastered.

The design requires readable text, touch controls that fit the reference phone, non-color selection cues, and instructions that work without audio. Tap-then-place is the intended alternative to dragging. The proposed 48 dp touch-target starting point and roughly 30 fps performance target are not measured compliance results.

Audio uses quiet ambience, readable confirmation cues, volume controls, and a silent-play path. Shipped audio and artwork need recorded ownership or license information. Generated concept images illustrate intentions and are not screenshots or import-ready Unity asset kits. See the art and UI brief.

Architecture boundaries​

The current technical addendum directs UI actions through View, Handler, and Service. Views display state and collect input. Handlers translate player actions into service requests. Services own application behavior and use the database layer for storage. Typed events notify interested components, and ServiceRegistry supplies shared providers. UI does not own SQL.

The older technical design names Game Manager, Progress Manager, Database Manager, SceneLoader, input, dialogue, and activity managers. Those names describe responsibilities and are not a command to create duplicate systems. Actual implementation must follow the existing Unity project conventions. The older blanket event-command diagram is subordinate to the newer addendum.

In the inspected database startup path, DatabaseManager validates and snapshots authored ScriptableObjects on the main thread. A queued worker creates or updates the SQLite tables and synchronizes definitions by stable identifier. That content synchronization is separate from saving a learner's progress and does not establish versioned migration support.

Only one activity owns gameplay input at a time. A purchase confirmation must update its related records together, then report success. Repeated submission or retry must not repeat the economic effect. Blockchain repair must read the same saved purchase instead of creating an unrelated example transaction.

The technical design and technical acceptance checklist define these boundaries.

This mapping is not a database dump from an accepted APK. The source review did not establish explicit versioned migrations or the complete v2 save model. Local username/password code does not change the approved simple demo-profile flow.

The core requires profile separation, build/content/schema versions, quest and encyclopedia progress, balances, inventory, ledger records, reward receipts, restoration flags, and supported checkpoints. The required semantics are not approved SQL table names. Christian owns the schema changes and their recovery tests.

A committed purchase must not leave the coins deducted without the item, or the item granted without its ledger record. A failed save must preserve the last good checkpoint. Switching profiles must clear the previous profile's state before loading another. In-place app updates need explicit save-preservation tests.

Set up and run the game​

Locations and prerequisites​

The game source is separate from this management repository. The documented remote is Kamiyon-Studio/Chainy-Game. The inspected local checkout is O:\Documents\Unity Projects\ChainyApp. This management checkout is O:\Documents\GitHub\Chainy. These are this workstation's paths, not required paths for every teammate. The local game checkout uses Plastic SCM and contains .plastic, not .git. Record its actual changeset identifier rather than inventing a Git commit for the local build.

Use the project's pinned Unity version instead of upgrading it during setup. The following is a setup procedure to verify, not a claim that a build was run for this handbook.

  1. Obtain the current game checkout and record its revision from Christian.
  2. Install Unity Hub and Unity Editor 6000.3.25f1.
  3. Include Android Build Support and its SDK, NDK, and OpenJDK modules.
  4. Add the folder containing Assets, Packages, and ProjectSettings to Unity Hub.
  5. Open the project and allow its declared packages to resolve.
  6. Check the Unity Console for compilation and import errors.
  7. Open Assets/Scenes/MainMenu.unity and press Play.
  8. Verify the actual available flow with synthetic local data.

First-time editor/package installation can need internet access. Git must be available for the Git-pinned SQLite package to resolve. That setup requirement is separate from offline gameplay on an installed device. No wallet account, cloud AI API key, or public blockchain connection is required for the core design.

The inspected editor input bindings use WASD or arrow keys for movement, E for interaction, Space for dialogue continuation, and Escape for pause. The input asset also declares J for the quest log, but a binding alone does not establish a working journal. Mobile source includes a floating joystick, contextual interaction, and tap-to-continue dialogue.

The current startup menu opens local login UI. In the inspected LoginUI.cs, success updates a feedback label without a visible call to open the main menu or world selection. The menu, level cards, and world-loading components exist separately, but their complete click path remains unverified. Record a blocked transition for Christian instead of assuming that sign-in reaches the designed chapter.

Use Window > General > Test Runner for Unity EditMode and PlayMode checks when applicable tests exist. The separate database-test README names a .csproj that was absent in the inspected checkout. Its zero-quest expectation also conflicts with the current one-quest configuration. That documented standalone test command must be repaired before it can provide verification.

Documentation and team tools​

The documentation viewer and board are management tools. Their availability does not affect the intended offline game loop.

The team board is the editing interface for cards, dates, named history, and handoffs. Use your assigned individual account and private password. Teammates do not need GitHub accounts to update the board. Credentials are deliberately omitted from this handbook.

Use Board for cards and Timeline for the Gantt view. Open a card to read its owner, prerequisites, completion check, evidence, and history. Record actual progress and blockers, then change status when the card's completion check is met. Create a separate handoff card when another role must continue the work.

GitHub issues receive queued copies of board changes. Do not independently edit managed issue bodies or old Project fields. docs/backlog.json is the publication snapshot, not a second editing interface. Trello is retired. Reference links can become public through issue copies, so they must not contain participant records, secrets, or private signed URLs.

Run the documentation viewer locally​

The viewer README specifies Node 24, npm, VitePress, Vue, Markdown tooling, and Mermaid. These are documentation dependencies, not dependencies installed on learner phones. From this workstation, the development command sequence is:

Set-Location 'O:\Documents\GitHub\Chainy\tools\docs-viewer'
npm ci
npm run dev

Use the local address printed by VitePress. The package script prepares the documents before starting the viewer. Its production validation command is npm run build, and its unit-test command is npm test. The optional search check is npm run test:search. Editing source Markdown does not publish the public site.

The repository also has board synchronization and deployment tooling. The board combines a static browser interface with a Cloudflare Worker and a SQLite Durable Object for staff accounts, cards, history, and the retry queue. This server-side SQLite store is separate from the game's on-device SQLite database. Maintainers can run npm ci, npm run check, and npm test from tools/kanban-sync. npm run deploy:check is its deployment dry run, not a deployment. Those tools are for the responsible maintainer, not prerequisites for reading this guide or playing the game. Use the viewer README, sync README, and board guide for their current operational details.

Testing and acceptance​

Specifications describe required results. Only executed checks against a named artifact establish acceptance. Every result needs the build revision, device, steps, expected result, actual result, tester, and appropriate evidence.

Test areaRequired observation
Installation and offline startThe named APK installs and launches on the reference phone without a gameplay network dependency
Movement and dialogueNearby interaction works; modal UI stops movement; closing restores input
EconomyReward 12 once, spend 8 plus 1, retain 3, own the lantern, and equip for free
Cancellation and duplicate inputCancellation is free; repeated taps and retries do not duplicate charges or rewards

PersistenceRelated records agree after success, failure, interruption, and repeat save
ProfilesTwo synthetic profiles remain separate through switching and relaunch
Recovery and updatesSafe error feedback, checkpoint recovery, and save-preserving signed update
InterfaceReadable text, non-color cues, usable touch controls, and silent-play instructions
Conditional AIPredictions depend on examples; practice and first reserved-final evidence stay separate

Measure loading, sustained performance, memory, heat, storage use, and suspend/resume behavior on the actual phone. Do not replace measurements with processor specifications or a desktop screenshot. No such performance measurements were made for this handbook.

Use V2-R01-V2-R12, the GDD's GP checks, and the QA plan for traceability. Deferred Wordle, BlockMatch, and teacher services are not core beta blockers. Missing core save integrity is a blocker even when the scene looks complete.

Common questions and troubleshooting​

Question or symptomAnswer or next action
Is Chainy a real crypto application?No. Currency, addresses, authorization, and ledger interactions are educational simulations.
Does the player need internet?The intended installed gameplay and saves are offline. Setup, later activation, transfer, updates, and project tools are separate jobs.
Is AI already working?Package presence does not establish that. The small classifier is conditional stretch, and runtime acceptance is unverified here.
Why is AI unavailable in a core demo?A01-A03 is admitted only after the core passes. It must not be presented as completed through concept art.
Is Blockchain repair BlockMatch?No. They are separate activities with separate rules.
Does a learner code recover another phone?No. It is neither authentication nor cross-device backup.
Does a restored workshop prove learning?It proves completion only when the build actually performs it. Learning requires appropriate evidence beyond completion.
Unity import or compile failsCheck the pinned editor version, declared packages, and Console errors. Record the exact error for Christian before changing versions.
The current game shows local account screensSource implementation and the approved beta flow differ. Record that mismatch; do not describe the account screen as the intended demo-profile flow.
Login or startup never finishesPreserve logs and synthetic reproduction steps. The November 7 review identified a database-startup-failure risk, but the current cause needs verification.
A save fails or progress appears wrongAvoid clearing data. Preserve the last good state and report build, profile, action, and observed result to Christian.
The APK will not installCapture the install error and check the build, SDK policy, ABI, storage, and signing compatibility. Do not erase an existing installation to bypass an unexplained failure.
A document disagrees with this guidePrefer direct owner decisions and the current baseline, then the canonical GDD and actual evidence. Report the discrepancy rather than silently changing scope.
