Chainy detailed game mechanics reference​

Preserved from the detailed GDD on October 8, 2026. The illustrated GDD is the main presentation document; this reference retains the implementation rules and traceability.

Version 0.4 • October 8, 2026 • English first • Design specification, not an implemented-build or school-approval claim.

Decision status: The baseline defines confirmed scope. The user confirms Images 1 and 2 as the definite visual direction; Image 3 supplies additional gameplay context only. Narrative, quantities, sample datasets, quiz thresholds, detailed controls and rules below are proposed implementation defaults unless expressly identified as confirmed. They make the design reviewable without pretending prototypes, approvals or playtests have occurred. October 8 user approval replaces CH-R01 exploration with direct map-to-lesson navigation and CH-R02 free construction with bounded ordering/linking. The user withdrew full A–Z coverage in favor of essential foundations. Other IDs remain traceable; no tested implementation is asserted.

Game Name​

Chainy: A 3D World Educational Game for learning emerging Technology (Blockchain & AI). Short product name: Chainy. The learner is an explorer restoring useful information and delivery services across a friendly technology world. BlockForge is a gameplay reference, not a replacement title.

Genre​

Offline single-player educational lesson-and-puzzle game for ages 12–17, especially indigent learners and underserved communities with limited exposure to technology. Its source-of-truth mission is foundational learning about Web3, Blockchain and AI, with every required lesson playable offline. Short guided activities teach through visible examples, experiments, explanations and retries. The cute presentation remains the user's chosen direction; reasoning and language suit the audience.

Game Elements​

Tap a world and lesson, read a short guide/example, select or sort choices, order/link supplied records, inspect inconsistencies, compare three copies, train/test a small classifier, configure a short agent policy, answer formative questions and earn persistent progress. Predicting, checking and revising supply meaningful choices. No prior ownership of a phone, wallet or online account is assumed.

Player​

One learner at a time on one Android device. A teacher-issued learner code selects an offline profile. Shared devices support explicit switching. The same code on another device does not restore progress or authenticate identity. No multiplayer or device-to-device networking is required for the three-node activity.

GAME PLAY​

You open Chainy without an internet connection and choose your local learner profile. The blue-haired guide in a yellow coat welcomes you to three places on a world map. In Web3 World, a short illustrated story asks whether a secret recovery phrase should be shared; you choose, see the consequence and explain a safer response. In Blockchain World, you inspect three delivery records, put them in order and link them. A received parcel with no earlier dispatch is highlighted, so you revise and test again. Another lesson shows a changed record and asks you to compare three saved copies. In the violet AI world, you label examples, train a small model and test a new parcel. A wrong prediction prompts you to inspect the examples and try again. You then help a robot deliver a parcel by arranging a short action sequence. Each lesson ends with a fresh understanding check and a saved progress summary before returning to the map.

Learning objectives and lesson content​

The beginner learning materials specify proposed teaching copy, examples and checks. Nine lessons are an authoring default to review against the working prototype and capacity, not a new user commitment. Foundational coverage supersedes full A–Z coverage. Encyclopedic brand/token detail, trading and advanced protocol topics are excluded from this capstone foundation scope.

Proposed prerequisites: W1, B1 and A1 are available at entry; each world advances sequentially (W1 → W2 → W3, B1 → B2 → B3, A1 → A2 → A3). Learners can choose a world and revisit completed lessons freely. W1 is suggested for first-time learners without blocking access to the others. Proposed 3–5 minutes per lesson is an untested design target, with breaks and replay allowed. Total lesson count/duration is not a research-session estimate. Practical scenarios introduce unfamiliar technology without assuming prior internet access or device fluency.

Key Features​

  • Coherent rounded 3D explorer/robot visual identity across Web3, Blockchain and AI worlds.
  • Direct map-to-lesson navigation and reusable selection, sorting, ordering and scenario activities.
  • Observable records, tampering and three on-device ledger copies.
  • A proposed real small example-trained classifier with separate test examples and understandable errors, conditional on a successful learning/device prototype.
  • A visible rule-based agent whose policy learners change.
  • Offline play, retryable feedback and locally saved badges/progress.
  • Teacher-mediated pseudonymous export separate from gameplay.

Design Guidelines​

Confirmed visual direction and accompanying media​

Image 1 — definite visual direction; user supplied. Preserve rounded toy-like 3D/chibi proportions, blue-haired explorer with yellow coat/backpack, friendly robot, soft light, chunky readable objects, green/blue islands, rounded panels and large controls. This is a reference, not a delivered screenshot or asset-count commitment. The walking/joystick scenes and two-world map are superseded interaction layouts; retain their art direction. Panel 7 motivates bounded ordering/linking. Panel 8 must explain the exact data/link/reference mismatch.

Image 2 — definite visual direction; user supplied. Preserve the violet AI city, cyan highlights, robot and visual continuity. Cat/dog imagery is a possible classification theme, not a computer-vision requirement. Replace panel 11's more-data claim with: “AI learns patterns from examples. Useful, correctly labeled examples can help; poor examples can lead to mistakes.” Panel 14 motivates the bounded agent route. Stars, coins, 3/6 or 3/12 counts and extra locks do not add scope; the current design has three worlds with a proposed short foundation sequence and saved progress/badges. The two-world layout is superseded.

Image 3 — gameplay/context only; user supplied, not visual design. Adapt records, links, ordering, tamper checks, quizzes and multiple copies into Chainy's style. Its flat cartoon look, BlockForge title, money, certificate, large network and extra levels are not requirements. “All data must match” needs a stated comparison rule; hashes reveal mismatches against references, not real-world truth.

Creative restrictions​

Keep the selected cute appearance with respectful English, practical scenarios and reasoning for ages 12–17. Avoid baby talk, long exposition and disruptive rewards. Proposed reusable production kit: one map, themed fixed lesson backdrops, one guide rig or pre-rendered guide set, shared blocks/nodes and reusable activity panels. Include a brief touch tutorial and plain-language terms for learners with limited technology exposure. Required instructions remain packaged text/icons; audio is optional, and no online lookup is necessary.

Proposed caps: three supplied records for the ordering/linking lesson, three fixed replicas, two classifier classes, one short fixed-grid route. Free placement, arbitrary component configuration, editable graph topology and walkable scenes are deferred by user approval. Preserve reasoning through prediction, explanation and fresh checkpoints. Reduce decorative variation before foundational learning objectives.

Recommended pre-final exclusions: real cryptocurrency/wallets, public blockchain, multiplayer, cloud AI, microphone, automatic cloud sync, teacher website, downloader, currency economy and certificates. No source caption adds authorization for extra scope. Use text/icons alongside color/audio; instructions work muted. Readability and touch/performance need real-device checks.

Game Design Definitions​

M-01 — Bootstrap and local profile (CH-R01, CH-R09; UI-01/02)​

ReferenceContract
O-01Application/bootstrap and local learner profile
P-01Supported content/schema; study/code; session state; committed checkpoint
A-01Validate/open; enter/select; confirm new profile; continue; switch
RulesGameplay requires usable content/schema/profile and session creation. Invalid code explains correction; unknown valid code warns of new empty profile. Code is not authentication.
RecoveryPreserve data on migration/schema failure; UI-12 recovery. Switch ends active session and clears prior learner state before another starts.

M-02 — Map and progression (CH-R08; UI-03)​

ReferenceContract
O-02Three-world map with short lesson cards
P-02Locked/available/in-progress/completed; prerequisite; committed badge/checkpoint
A-02Inspect; start; continue; replay
RulesThe entry lessons W1/B1/A1 are initially available; the versioned curriculum defines sequential prerequisites within each world. This prerequisite organization is a proposed default. Locked cards explain prerequisites. Replay starts a distinct session and preserves first attempts. All required lessons in that frozen curriculum complete → journey summary; map remains usable. Never infer completion from a fixed count embedded in UI code.

M-03 — Guided briefing and foundation scenarios (CH-R01, CH-R08; UI-04)​

ReferenceContract
O-03Guide panel, illustrated example, versioned scenario/options
P-03Lesson/objective ID; current panel; selected response; explanation; committed checkpoint
A-03Read/advance; inspect detail; select/sort; submit; inspect feedback; revise; open next activity; pause
RulesBriefing presents one objective, a short example and a visible action. Scenario correctness and explanatory feedback come from reviewed content. Repeated taps cannot duplicate submissions. A fresh checkpoint uses a different scenario/example from the worked example. No walking, collection search or precision movement prerequisite.
Feedback/recoveryShow result, reason and next action in plain language and text/icons. Checkpoint restores the lesson state. Missing packaged content is a blocking defect, not learner failure.

Proposed first-use tutorial: tap a highlighted lesson card, read/advance the example, select an option and submit; show how to return or replay. W1–W3 use this common scenario/sort template. Touch fluency is supported without testing prior technology exposure. Exact copy/checks are versioned in the learning materials; fictional secrets are never entered as real credentials.

M-04 — Bounded ordering and linking (CH-R02; UI-05)​

ReferenceContract
O-04Fixed workbench, supplied records, ordered slots and predecessor selectors
P-04Record IDs; sequence; predecessor references; selected record; undo state
A-04Inspect; select; move earlier/later or optional drag; assign predecessor; undo; test; reset
RulesUse the supplied record set once each. Position is a sequence choice; no arbitrary spatial placement, component palette or editable node graph. Selected predecessor links are checked explicitly against the sequence and digest contract. Explain missing, duplicate, wrong-order or broken-link results.
RecoveryUndo restores the previous edit. Reset confirms and restores the scenario without deleting profile history. Test is a meaningful attempt; every touch is not evidence.

Select a record → inspect its event → move earlier/later → select predecessor → Test. Buttons provide a drag-free alternative. Guided demonstration precedes an independent record-order problem. This intentionally replaces the earlier free-construction acceptance criterion by the user's October 8 approval; prior sandbox illustrations are historical concepts.

M-05 — Records and ordering (CH-R03, CH-R08; UI-05)​

ReferenceContract
O-05Versioned delivery records in linked blocks
P-05ID, event/payload, predecessor reference, computed digest, ordering constraints
A-05Inspect; assign; link; compute/verify; revise
ValidatorExactly three required records once each; one connected acyclic sequence, one start/end; PACK before DISPATCH before RECEIVE; predecessor reference equals previous block's full digest. First uses declared genesis reference.
OutcomesValid; missing/duplicate record; wrong order; missing/broken predecessor; invalid topology. Highlight violated rule; remain editable.

Synthetic CH-L1-ORDER-01: P01 packs parcel K7 at A; D01 dispatches K7 from A to B; R01 receives K7 at B. Correct logical order P01 → D01 → R01. Scenario times 09:00/09:10/09:20 may be displayed; actual device clocks never decide correctness. P01 → R01 → D01 fails because receive needs prior dispatch. Sequence slots show logical order; decorative screen coordinates are not part of the record validation rule.

Canonical serialization and full digests govern checks; shortened display values never do. Freeze field order/encoding/hash contract with the technical owner before agents implement it. One scaffold demonstrates a link, then learners independently order and link the supplied records.

M-06 — Tamper detection and integrity (CH-R04; UI-06)​

ReferenceContract
O-06Working ledger and immutable trusted scenario checkpoint
P-06Current payload/digest; predecessor references; trusted version; comparison
A-06Inspect; introduce test alteration; verify; compare; restore; retest
ValidatorRecompute working digest, inspect successor references and compare with trusted scenario checkpoint. Explain internal link consistency and reference agreement separately.
OutcomesUnchanged; data mismatch; broken link; missing trusted reference. Missing reference cannot establish historical correctness.

Synthetic CH-L2-TAMPER-01: change D01 destination B to C. Verification detects a changed digest and R01's stale link. Recompute every following hash: links can now be internally consistent, but the chain still differs from the trusted B destination. Learner identifies the change, restores the supplied trusted value and verifies. Say “This differs from our saved exercise reference,” not “A hash proves this delivery happened.” Later blocks remain visible; change does not erase stored blocks. A maliciously replaced trusted reference is outside the exercise's assurance.

M-07 — Three-replica synchronization (CH-R05; UI-06)​

ReferenceContract
O-07Replica nodes A, B, C on one device
P-07Ledger per node; internal validity; comparison signature; repair candidate
A-07Inspect fixed node cards; introduce mismatch; compare; preview repair; confirm
RuleStated normal exercise assumption: at most one corrupted copy. Two identical internally valid copies propose a candidate. One differing copy is repaired only after preview/confirmation. The three node cards are provided in a fixed layout. All-different or insufficient valid matches → no automatic repair.
FeedbackAgreement/disagreement per node; candidate source and exact changed fields. Label as simplified classroom rule, not secure real-world consensus.

Synthetic CH-L2-REPLICAS-01: A/B contain P01,D01,R01 with B destination; C contains destination C. A+B propose B under the one-corrupt-copy assumption. A separate stress case deliberately breaks that assumption: three different versions show “No agreement—inspect the trusted checkpoint.” It teaches the boundary of the classroom rule, not a case the one-corrupt-copy promise can resolve. An explicitly selected provided checkpoint resolves that case. Completion requires the three replicas matching the scenario reference after explicit reconciliation and a learner explanation; arbitrary shared corruption is insufficient. No mining/network transport or proof of distributed security is implied.

M-08 — Example-trained classification (CH-R06; UI-07)​

Prototype gate: this remains the selected proposed implementation, not a working feature. Before production content multiplication, verify changed training data changes held-out predictions, learner feedback is understandable, and the offline phone prototype works. A failed prototype triggers an explicit scope decision; do not silently substitute hard-coded answers while claiming training.

ReferenceContract
O-08Training examples, two class prototypes, held-out test parcels
P-08Normalized numeric feature vector; learner label; training/test membership; hidden content truth; fitted version; squared distances
A-08Inspect; label; train; predict unseen item; reveal/evaluate; revise; retrain
RuleProposed local C# nearest-centroid model: mean vector per class, smaller squared Euclidean distance predicts class. At least one valid example per required class; test items excluded from fit. Out-of-range/invalid features reject fitting. Distance difference within proposed 0.000001 tolerance → Ambiguous.
OutcomesNot trained; insufficient classes/examples; invalid features; prediction; ambiguous tie. Edits make model stale until retrained. Show distances, never an invented probability.

Synthetic CH-L3-CLASSIFY-01: fictional parcels have weight and fragility, each a scenario feature normalized to 0–1, not sensor/image input. Standard examples (0.2,0.2), (0.4,0.2) give centroid (0.3,0.2); Careful examples (0.6,0.8), (0.8,0.8) give (0.7,0.8). Held-out T1=(0.6,0.6), content truth Careful, has squared distances 0.25 and 0.05 and predicts Careful. T2=(0.5,0.5) has 0.13 to both and returns Ambiguous.

Designed error: relabel (0.6,0.8) Standard and retrain. Standard becomes (0.4,0.4), Careful (0.8,0.8); T1 ties at 0.08. Another held-out parcel, T3=(0.55,0.55), has content truth Careful. The clean model's distances are 0.185 and 0.085, predicting Careful; the mislabeled model's distances become 0.045 and 0.125, incorrectly predicting Standard. Correcting the label restores Careful. This demonstrates both ambiguity and an actual wrong prediction caused by changed examples, without claiming more examples always improve a model. Content truth is a synthetic scenario label, not independently established real-world truth. Hide it before first prediction. Across A1/A2, completion requires training/testing, inspecting the designed error/tie, revision/retraining and recorded comparison. A1 requires initial fit and held-out testing; A2 begins with reviewed packaged examples or a validated saved checkpoint and requires the error investigation/revision. Record attempts against the active lesson ID; do not require all A2 actions to complete A1. Repeated clicks without changed examples are insufficient. Quiz assesses interpretation, not perfect accuracy.

Pending theme choice: parcels remain the existing default. Image 2's cats/dogs may replace the theme after choosing teachable explicit features and reviewed data. That must not silently turn the activity into computer vision or hard-coded sorting. Theme affects final content/art; example-trained behavior stays required.

M-09 — Observe–decide–act delivery agent (CH-R07; UI-08)​

ReferenceContract
O-09Agent, bounded grid, parcel, destination, obstacle
P-09Cell/orientation; carrying; visible ordered policy; observation; command index; steps/trace
A-09Add/reorder/remove Move, Turn Left/Right, Pick Up, Deliver, Stop; optional one If obstacle ahead then Turn Right; step/run/stop/reset
RuleProposed 12-command/20-executed-step caps. Each dispatched command consumes one step. An optional If command turns right when the next cell is blocked, otherwise leaves position/facing unchanged; either case advances to the next command, with no implicit loop. Actions update deterministically. Blocked/out-of-grid Move stops; Pick Up requires parcel here and empty hands; Deliver requires carrying at destination. Exhausting commands without delivery is stopped incomplete.
OutcomesRunning; delivered; blocked; invalid action; stopped incomplete; step limit. Retain trace and editable policy.

Synthetic CH-L3-AGENT-01: 4×3 grid, x=0–3 eastward and y=0–2 northward. Start (0,0) facing east, parcel (1,0), obstacle (2,0), destination (1,2). Success: Move, Pick Up, Turn Left, Move, Move, Deliver. Move again after pickup hits obstacle and stops at (1,0), still carrying. Trace shows observation → action → state, motivating the turn. Reset restores agent/parcel while retaining policy; separate Clear Policy confirms deletion. Delivered is set only by the carrying/destination rule. This is visible rule-based behavior, not generative AI or reinforcement learning.

M-10 — Formative quiz (CH-R08; UI-09)​

ReferenceContract
O-10Versioned question set and submitted answer
P-10Objective/item ID; first answer; current attempt; correctness; hints/retries; gate
A-10Read; select; submit; inspect explanation; retry
RuleProposed three distinct questions per lesson; all three need a saved response and at least two distinct questions must be correct under their latest saved answers, plus all required activities complete. Retrying one item replaces only its current gate result and never counts the same item twice; original answers remain immutable evidence. This is a tuning default, not a school pass mark or validated research instrument. Disable repeat submit while saving. A deliberate new answer is a new attempt.
FeedbackExplain correct and wrong choices. Preserve first-attempt evidence and assistance separately from eventual completion.

Review items: W1–W3—who controls information, public versus secret information, safe response to suspicious requests. B1–B3—why receive follows dispatch, predecessor meaning, altered-and-rehashed records, copy disagreement, hashes versus truth. A1–A3—training versus test examples, incorrect labels, ambiguous cases, checking predictions, observation justifying an agent turn. Educator review supplies final distractors/copy. Research pre/post instruments are separately versioned.

M-11 — Persistence, checkpoints and rewards (CH-R08, CH-R09; UI-10/11)​

ReferenceContract
O-11SQLite attempt, session, progress checkpoint, lesson badge
P-11Stable event/session IDs; immutable build/content version; first/retry result; checkpoint; completion
A-11Commit; retry same save; resume; replay; show summary
RuleAttempt/checkpoint/completion commit together. Only successful commit displays badge/unlock. Failed-save retry reuses event ID; deliberate gameplay retry uses a new one. One badge per learner/lesson; later attempts stay separate.
RecoveryBusy/full/failed save explains retry; no false success or silent deletion. Restart after process loss marks unfinished session interrupted and resumes in a new session.

Checkpoint defaults: lesson start after session creation; briefing/scenario milestones; activity entry; successful activity completion; committed quiz result; lesson completion. Store logical state, not animations. Uncommitted edits may be lost; exit/resume copy explains this. Android pause requests a safe checkpoint but is not the sole save trigger. Incompatible/missing checkpoints explain safe restart while retaining historical evidence. Failed answers remain evidence and never become retrospectively correct.

M-12 — Staff export and recovery (CH-R10, CH-R11; UI-12)​

ReferenceContract
O-12Selected study/session scope, export, receipt
P-12Count; schema/build/content versions; export ID; integrity digest; written/receipt states
A-12Preview; export through Android picker; cancel/retry; transfer externally; inspect importer counts; record receipt
RuleConsistent logical snapshot with approved pseudonymous fields. Cancel/failure preserves records. File saved differs from transferred/received. Re-import deduplicates stable IDs; malformed/conflicting records rejected/quarantined.
RecoveryRetry without delete-on-export. Signed in-place update must retain tested records. Code cannot recover uninstalled data.

Identity maps, participant exports, credentials and signing keys stay out of repositories/issues. Teacher-held identity mapping makes evidence pseudonymous. Staff navigation/optional convenience PIN is not strong authentication. Custody, transfer and retention need the approved offline evidence plan.

Layout and control mapping​

Proposed landscape layout: lesson title/objective above the activity, Pause in a consistent corner, guide/explanation in a readable panel and a clearly labeled primary action below. Fixed activity views leave space for records, examples or the agent grid. Test and Back are separated from Reset; selected-record details must not cover links or choices. Dialogue has speaker, short text and explicit advance. No joystick is needed.

InputMap/briefing/scenarioSpecialist activity
TapChoose lesson, read/advance, select option, inspectSelect record/predecessor/label/command and labeled action.
Earlier/later buttonsUsed only for a sort activityReorder records/commands without precision dragging.
DragOptional sorting convenienceOptional reorder with equivalent buttons.
Android BackClose modal or pauseConfirm loss of uncommitted edits; never bypass save.
PauseSafe boundaryPreserve editable state and recovery-aware exit.

Proposed minimum 48 dp targets with spacing need phone testing. Distinguish pressed/selected/disabled states. Larger text, wrapping and safe areas must not hide buttons. Digests get readable detail views; color themes complement text/icon correctness.

Worst-layout review checks: thumbs cover targets; reset beside submit; panel hides links; long English text pushes buttons off-screen; color-only mismatch disappears; dragging requires precision; Android Back silently discards work. Test with two hands, muted audio and larger text. Observe handedness before adding remapping scope.

Feedback follows outcome → rule/observation → next inspection → revise. Example: “Receive has no earlier dispatch. Inspect the dispatch record and its link.” Hint escalation goes from concept reminder to worked example; it never silently changes the learner's answer.
