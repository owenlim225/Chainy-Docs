Chainy game design: island lesson collection​

This is the working game design for the owner's November 11, 2026 direction. It replaces the economy-led progression direction and incorporates the owner's subsequent confirmations: prerequisite order, three distinct minigames per world, sequential world unlocking, a required quiz, and offline AI teaching simulations. Detailed rules identified as implementation defaults are design recommendations chosen to make the specification executable; they are not additional owner confirmations. It describes the complete intended game, not a claim that the game is implemented or can be delivered within the remaining calendar time. Documentation and preparation only: Unity development waits for the owner's explicit final approval.Start here · Decisions · Lesson source pack

1. The game in one paragraph​

Chainy is an offline, single-player Android exploration game for ages 12–17, initially in English. The player selects an unlocked world, starting with Web3, explores a compact 3D island, and follows 13 ordered lesson quests. Each lesson leads to a collectible learning object or a distinct minigame encounter. Completing the lesson records its term in that world's encyclopedia collection and activates the next quest. Recording all 13 terms finishes its collection and opens a required multiple-choice review. Completing that review finishes the world and unlocks the next one: Web3 → Blockchain → AI.

The existing source pack supplies concepts and draft assessment seeds. Its older five-clusters-per-world sequence, compulsory per-term quizzes, shared-purchase prerequisite, and AI-as-stretch framing do not govern this new gameplay direction. Sean remains the documentation owner; these drafts have not received educator validation or established learning gains.

3. Player journey​

Each world supplies its own fictional examples and explanations. No lesson assumes that the player has bought an item or studied a term in a different world. World unlocking controls access; lesson examples remain self-contained and do not require a shared purchase or transferable inventory.

Recommended encyclopedia discovery display: locked slots show their letter and “Not recorded”; explanatory content unlocks only after lesson completion. World categories can be browsed from any island, but browsing never grants completion. The quest order and encyclopedia's A–Z display order are separate: teaching can follow prerequisites while the collection remains alphabetical.

5. Tutorial and exploration rules​

On the first visit to each world, the spawn area directs the player to its tutorial NPC. The player may move to the NPC, but lesson interactions stay locked until orientation finishes. The tutorial introduces movement, Interact, the active quest, collection, encyclopedia, and pause in short steps. Recommended interaction: ask the player to open and close Quest and Encyclopedia once, then finish orientation. Save tutorial completion separately for each world; offer the controls reminder on later visits. Closing before tutorial completion repeats orientation on return without granting lesson progress.

Retain the current visual direction: fixed orthographic isometric camera, restrained low-poly scenery, blue-haired yellow-clad Explorer, and screen-faced guides. Retain Sean as the Web3 guide, Matthew as the Blockchain guide, and Lee as the AI guide unless the owner changes them. Use a small island layout with visible landmarks and short routes, without jumping, combat, precision platforming, or a maze. Each current quest names its landmark and target object or NPC; a clearly distinguishable marker can guide exploration.

6. Lesson and collection rules​

The following checkpoint details are proposed implementation rules for the confirmed loop.

Lesson typeOrdered interactionCompletion
Ordinary collectionReach the active lesson object → Interact → inspect explanation/example → RecordSave the lesson completion and encyclopedia entry together
Minigame lessonFollow its item/NPC/facility steps → launch its scene → satisfy its success rule → view explanationSave minigame success, lesson completion, and encyclopedia entry together, then return to the island

Only the current lesson can advance. Interacting with a later lesson shows a hint to finish the current quest and leaves the object available. Earlier recorded objects may show their entry again; they never increment the count twice. Ordinary lessons do not insert an extra compulsory quiz or require touching the object accidentally. Record confirms collection, not that the learner read, understood, or mastered the explanation.

Minigame prerequisite objects are temporary quest evidence, not an inventory/equipment system. Store their completed step flags; show the acquired item in the quest log rather than adding inventory management. An out-of-order NPC or facility interaction explains the missing step without launching the scene. No required item can be sold, spent, dropped, or permanently lost.

Each successful lesson activates exactly the next lesson in the same world. The completion transaction updates the entry, lesson, next objective, and collection-complete flag consistently. It does not mark the world complete or unlock the next world before the required quiz is resolved and saved. Until saving succeeds, show an explicit pending/error state and retain a retry route. The object may visibly change to Recorded only when the checkpoint succeeds.

8. Nine distinct minigames​

The owner confirmed three distinct minigames in each world. The nine scene assignments below are implementation defaults with different player rules; shared loading, pause, input, feedback, and save code is appropriate. There is no minigame selection screen that bypasses its lesson encounter.

Safe Address Handoff: LC-W05​

After LC-W01–04, collect the fictional address card, speak to the wallet guide, then use the dummy terminal. The separate MG_SafeAddressHandoff scene is a routing puzzle, with a visible instruction that only the receiving address may leave the device. Three cards show a fictional receiving address, private signing key, and recovery phrase; all strings are visibly dummy examples and never resemble valid credentials. Tap the address card, then the Outgoing message slot; tap each secret card, then the Private storage slot. Submit the arrangement, then confirm the outgoing preview contains only the address. Success requires all three placements and the safe preview confirmation. An incorrect arrangement highlights which information would be exposed, keeps the puzzle open, and lets the player move that card. No network request, scanning, balance, payment, recovery flow, or actual wallet is present. After successful saving, record W and return beside the terminal with LC-W06 active.

Price Path: LC-W10​

After LC-W01–09, collect a chart strip, speak to the market guide, then use the history display. The separate MG_PricePath scene is a graph-reading activity using fictional units and no trading choices. The player taps two time markers to select the largest adjacent change on each of two labeled charts: chart A has values 10, 12, 7, 8; chart B has values 6, 6, 9, 8. Chart A's correct interval is time 2–3 with a change of 5; chart B's is time 2–3 with a change of 3. Use displayed numerical differences rather than requiring rapid mental arithmetic. The final future marker is blank; choose “Unknown: past changes do not guarantee the next value” instead of an asserted rise or fall. Success requires both intervals and the uncertainty statement; mistakes display the selected values and permit reselection. After successful saving, record V and return beside the display with LC-W11 active.

Message Inspector: LC-W12​

After LC-W01–11, collect a fictional envelope, speak to the kiosk attendant, then interact with the message kiosk. The separate MG_MessageInspector scene presents four fictional requests. For each, choose Continue, Verify, or Reject, then select the evidence supporting that choice.

RequestExpected actionEvidence
Known island guide asks the player to read a public island noticeContinueKnown source, public information, no secret or payment requested
Someone requests the player's secret to unlock free coinsRejectRequest for a secret
An unknown poster promises a prize through a linkVerifySource/destination is unverified; no real link is opened
A message claims an asset's future value is guaranteedRejectUnsupported guarantee

Success requires the correct action and evidence for all four requests. An incorrect choice explains the overlooked evidence and allows retrying that request. The lesson teaches judgment about the displayed information; Continue is not a universal guarantee that a real message is safe. After successful saving, record the R entry and return the player beside the kiosk with LC-W13 active.

Archive Reconstruction: LC-B02​

After LC-B01, collect a reference slip, speak to the archivist, then use the archive workstation. The separate MG_ArchiveReconstruction scene shows checkpoint C0, three block cards, and a trusted reference for a fictional record field. The introductory panel explains a previous-block reference before asking the player to arrange the cards. Each card has a visible ID and previous-reference label: B1 follows C0, B2 follows B1, and B3 follows B2. Tap-select a card, tap its slot, and submit the order. Then compare two versions of a field against the trusted reference and select the altered field. Finally choose the explanation that a mismatch requires investigation against that reference.

Success requires the order, altered-field selection, and explanation to be correct. Feedback points to the specific mismatched reference or field and permits correction. This is a local record-linking and comparison exercise; labels are not cryptographic hashes, and a correct arrangement does not create consensus or prove historical truth. After successful saving, record the B entry and return beside the workstation with LC-B03 active.

Test the Helper: LC-A09​

After LC-A01–08, collect a fresh-case pack, speak to the lab guide, then use the test station. The separate MG_TestHelper scene prominently says “Scripted AI teaching simulation.” It presents three unfamiliar fictional delivery cases, the verified case details, and authored helper predictions. For each case, reveal the prediction, decide whether it matches the verified case, and correct any wrong category.

Verified exampleAuthored predictionExpected review
Complete address and matching destination; verified RoutineRoutineMatches
Destination mismatch; verified ReviewRoutineIncorrect; change to Review
Missing address; verified ReviewReviewMatches

The final choice asks why checking only familiar training examples is insufficient: new cases may expose mistakes. Success requires correct review of all three cases and that explanation. Feedback contrasts prediction with evidence and allows retry; no real training occurs in this candidate. These practice cases are not a validated evaluation set and the results do not measure a real model's reliability. After successful saving, record the V entry and return beside the test station with LC-A10 active. Actual model training is outside this confirmed simulation-based implementation.

Condition Machine: LC-B09​

After LC-B01–08, collect a rule card, talk to the facility keeper, then use the condition console. The separate MG_ConditionMachine scene is a Boolean input-and-output puzzle, labeled “Contract-condition illustration; not a deployed smart contract.” Its displayed rule is “Open the demonstration locker only when permission is valid AND the request is unused.” For the three rounds, fixed inputs are valid/unused, valid/already used, and invalid/unused. Before running the illustration, select the expected Open or Stay closed lamp; the correct outputs are Open, Stay closed, Stay closed. After each selection, Run highlights both conditions and shows how the output follows the stated rule. Success requires all three correct predictions; an incorrect prediction receives the trace and retries that round. The example does not execute payment, mutate a wallet, or imply that all real contracts use this rule. After successful saving, record S and return beside the console with LC-B10 active.

Minimum Disclosure: LC-B13​

After LC-B01–12, collect a sealed fictional credential, speak to the privacy guide, then use the proof exhibit. The separate MG_MinimumDisclosure scene is a disclosure puzzle, prominently labeled “Concept illustration; no cryptographic proof is generated.” Each round shows a verifier's question, a closed credential, an authored verification seal, and optional detail tiles. Round one asks only whether an invented resident meets an age threshold; choose the Meets threshold seal while leaving exact birthday and full name covered. Round two asks only whether an invented visitor has entry permission; choose the Permission confirmed seal while leaving the private credential number and home address covered. The seal is explicitly assumed to come from a suitable verification system; hiding text alone does not prove a claim. Submit succeeds only when the requested seal is selected and every unneeded detail remains covered, then choose “The verifier checks the requested claim without receiving the underlying details.” Unnecessary disclosure or a wrong seal gives specific feedback and resets only the current round. After successful saving, record Z, return beside the exhibit, and offer the required Blockchain quiz because all 13 entries are now recorded.

Label Lab: LC-A04​

After LC-A01–03, collect the example tray, talk to the lab assistant, then use the labeling desk. The separate MG_LabelLab scene asks the player to attach known-answer stickers to four fictional training examples. Instructions show that a verified complete matching address is Routine, while a verified mismatch or missing address is Review. The four cases are complete/matching, missing address, complete/mismatched, and another complete/matching address; expected labels are Routine, Review, Review, Routine. Tap a sticker and then its example; submit the completed sheet. Incorrect stickers receive an evidence explanation and can be replaced individually. Success requires all four known labels and the final choice “A label is the known answer supplied with an example.” The scene states that this activity prepares labeled examples; it does not train a model or claim the player generated predictions. After successful saving, record L and return beside the desk with LC-A05 active.

Fact Patch: LC-A12​

After LC-A01–11, collect the reference sheet, talk to the editor NPC, then use the text exhibit. The separate MG_FactPatch scene is a sentence-repair activity labeled “Authored example of an AI response; no live generation.” The reference sheet says “Museum opens at 09:00; entry is free; delivery desk closes at 16:00.” The authored response says “Museum opens at 08:00; entry is free; delivery desk closes at 16:00; visitors must send a password.” Tap the incorrect opening time and replace it with 09:00 from the reference; keep the two supported clauses; remove the unsupported password instruction. The final response must contain exactly the three supported clauses and no password request. Choose the explanation “Generated-looking text can contain errors or unsupported instructions; compare it with trusted information.” Success requires the corrected response and explanation; feedback identifies the unsupported clause and allows editing without restarting the scene. After successful saving, record G and return beside the exhibit with LC-A13 active.

Shared scene lifecycle​

Save completed prerequisite steps before launch and remember the originating world, lesson, and safe return point. Disable repeated launch taps while loading; a load failure leaves the quest available to retry. Pause stops the activity; exit returns to the island without awarding that lesson. Completed prerequisite steps remain saved, so the player need not collect the same envelope/reference/case pack again. An unfinished minigame restarts its round on re-entry; mid-round resume is not promised. No timer, lives, spending, or permanent failure is required. A successful completion saves once before returning; if it fails, retain the result in memory and offer Retry Save or explicit discard of unsaved completion. If the app closes before a successful save, the lesson remains incomplete and the activity can be replayed. If the save succeeded but return/loading was interrupted, resume the island with the lesson already recorded. Revisiting a completed activity through its island encounter never creates a second entry or advances unrelated lessons.

9. Encyclopedia, completion, and review quiz​

The encyclopedia is a collection record of 39 entries, divided into the three 13-entry categories. Each recorded entry has its letter, exact source term, short meaning, example, and source attribution. Use “Recorded” and “World complete” rather than “Mastered.” Unrecorded entries do not count toward completion; a title visible in the quest log is not a collected entry.

A world's collection completes when its 13 required lesson entries have been successfully saved. Offer its required quiz immediately after that save, including when the thirteenth lesson is a minigame. The player may postpone the quiz to explore or leave, but the world remains “Quiz required” and the next world stays locked. The world completes only when the quiz is resolved and that completion is successfully saved. Unlock Blockchain after Web3 completion and AI after Blockchain completion; new profiles start with only Web3 available. Completed worlds remain available for exploration, encyclopedia review, and replays. Finishing AI produces “Journey complete: 39 entries recorded and three world reviews completed,” without claiming mastery or measured learning improvement.

The owner confirmed 13 required questions per world, one for each lesson, with correction retries until all are correct. Section 15 defines every required question in lesson order. Four options, one correct answer, no timer, and no percentage threshold are the specified presentation rules. On the first submission for an item, record whether it was correct, then show the explanatory reason. For a wrong response, keep that item active, allow the learner to consult the encyclopedia, and ask them to choose again until it is correct. Only a correct choice enables Next; all 13 must be resolved before Finish can save review completion and unlock the next world. The result shows “First responses: X/13; review completed after corrections,” keeping initial performance distinct from assisted correction. If Finish saving fails, retain the answers and show Retry Save; the next world remains locked until the transaction succeeds.

Leaving early records an abandoned attempt separately and preserves encyclopedia progress; it grants no quiz completion or unlock. An abandoned quiz restarts all 13 questions on return; partial first responses remain part of that abandoned attempt, not a completed score. Replay creates a new attempt and preserves previous completion and unlocks even if replay is abandoned. No quiz result is represented as school grading, proof of learning gain, or a validated assessment score.

10. Persistence, recovery, and accessibility​

Keep SQLite as the offline store and retain the simple local profile approach. No sign-in, teacher service, or network request is required to play. The minimal logical state is profile ID, content/schema version, per-world tutorial completion, active lesson/step, completed lesson IDs, encyclopedia entries, and separate quiz attempt/results including required-review completion. Collection completion derives from the 13 specified recorded lesson IDs, not raw interaction counts. World completion additionally requires a saved completed required review; access to the next world derives from that state. Save quiz completion and its unlock atomically so the world selector and persisted access cannot disagree. Minigame configuration and curriculum are authored content, not data supplied by a learner.

Store lesson completion and its encyclopedia entry atomically; duplicate taps and retries must be idempotent. On relaunch, use the last successful checkpoint and a safe island spawn near its objective, not an exact animation frame. Pause/encyclopedia/quest/dialogue overlays suspend world interaction and clear held joystick input before resuming. Android backgrounding pauses input and audio, attempts a safe checkpoint, and preserves the previous checkpoint if saving fails. Switching profiles clears the previous profile's in-memory progress before loading the next profile. Unknown or incompatible saved content must show a recovery message and preserve the old data rather than silently reset it. Existing saves are not automatically converted to 39-lesson completion; Christian must define and test any migration after reviewing the current schema.

Use readable text on the Android reference phone, large touch targets, text/icon feedback in addition to color, and equivalent essential information with sound muted. Use tap-to-select/place controls where dragging would require precision. The current baseline retains API 25 minimum installation and Android 10/API 29 reference testing; actual compatibility and performance require a named build and device checks.

11. Excluded systems​

No functioning economy, shop, trading, real wallet, cryptocurrency transaction, seed entry, public blockchain, or live exchange is needed. Fixed examples may explain fees, wallets, tokens, or contract conditions without implementing those services. No general inventory/equipment system, combat, multiplayer, competitive leaderboard, paid hints, cloud chatbot, or standalone Wordle/BlockMatch mode is required by this design. Teacher collection, transfer, upload, and app updates remain separate from offline gameplay.
