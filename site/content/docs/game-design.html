GAME DESIGN DOCUMENT (GDD): Chainy​

Version 0.3 • October 8, 2026 • English first • Design specification, not an implemented-build or school-approval claim.

Decision status: The baseline defines confirmed scope. The user confirms Images 1 and 2 as the definite visual direction; Image 3 supplies additional gameplay context only. Narrative, quantities, sample datasets, quiz thresholds, detailed controls and rules below are proposed implementation defaults unless expressly identified as confirmed. They make the design reviewable without pretending prototypes, approvals or playtests have occurred. Existing CH-R01–12 meanings are preserved.

Game Name​

Chainy. The learner is an explorer restoring useful information and delivery services across a friendly technology world. BlockForge is a gameplay reference, not a replacement title.

Genre​

Offline single-player 3D educational exploration and construction puzzle game for ages 13–17. Blockchain and AI concepts are taught through visible systems, experiments, explanations and retries. The cute presentation remains the user's chosen direction; reasoning and language suit the audience.

Game Elements​

Explore compact areas, talk to robot guides, find records and examples, freely place/configure components, connect/test systems, detect/repair inconsistencies, train a small classifier, configure a bounded delivery policy, answer formative questions and earn persistent badges. Discovery supplies a reason to investigate; construction and revision supply meaningful choices.

Player​

One learner at a time on one Android device. A teacher-issued learner code selects an offline profile. Shared devices support explicit switching. The same code on another device does not restore progress or authenticate identity. No multiplayer or device-to-device networking is required for the three-node activity.

GAME PLAY​

You enter a bright island town wearing a yellow explorer coat, with blue hair and a small backpack. A friendly robot points toward a station whose delivery records no longer tell a clear story. You walk between stone paths, collect three records and read what happened to a parcel. At the workbench you place blocks wherever you can inspect them, put records inside and draw predecessor links. Your first test highlights a received parcel that was never dispatched. You revise the links, test again and watch the chain verify. Later, a changed record and disagreeing copies challenge you to explain what evidence can be trusted. Across the map, the violet AI district invites you to label examples, train a small model and inspect a prediction that goes wrong. You revise the examples, then guide a delivery robot by changing its visible policy. Each lesson ends with an explanation, a short quiz and a saved badge before returning to the map.

Learning objectives and level content​

LevelObjectiveRequired actionsCompletion evidence
L1 — Records and LinksExplain records, causal ordering and predecessor linksCollect three synthetic records; freely place/configure/link blocks; verify (M-03–05)Valid sequence plus quiz; explain why receiving follows dispatch and what a link represents.
L2 — Verify and RepairDetect changed data and compare copies without confusing consistency with truthInspect/alter/repair ledger (M-06); arrange/connect three replicas, inspect disagreement and reconcile (M-07)Both exercises plus quiz; explain trusted reference and classroom agreement assumption.
L3 — Examples and DecisionsExplain how examples affect predictions and how an agent chooses actionsLabel/train/test/revise classifier (M-08); edit/run visible delivery policy (M-09)Classifier investigation, successful delivery and quiz; explain a wrong prediction and an observe–decide–act trace.

Proposed order L1 → L2 → L3; completed levels remain replayable. Proposed gameplay budget 30–45 minutes, roughly 8–12 minutes per level plus transitions/breaks. This is untested and differs from a full research session.

Key Features​

  • Cute coherent 3D explorer/robot world with blockchain and AI areas.
  • Free placement/configuration/connections within bounded workbenches and multiple valid layouts.
  • Observable records, tampering and three on-device ledger copies.
  • A real small example-trained classifier with separate test examples and understandable errors.
  • A visible rule-based agent whose policy learners change.
  • Offline play, retryable feedback and locally saved badges/progress.
  • Teacher-mediated pseudonymous export separate from gameplay.

Design Guidelines​

Confirmed visual direction and accompanying media​

Image 1 — definite visual direction; user supplied. Preserve rounded toy-like 3D/chibi proportions, blue-haired explorer with yellow coat/backpack, friendly robot, soft light, chunky readable objects, green/blue islands, rounded panels and large controls. This is a reference, not a delivered screenshot or asset-count commitment. Panel 7 must become freely configurable construction rather than one correct-slot arrangement. Panel 8 must explain the exact data/link/reference mismatch.

Image 2 — definite visual direction; user supplied. Preserve the violet AI city, cyan highlights, robot and visual continuity. Cat/dog imagery is a possible classification theme, not a computer-vision requirement. Replace panel 11's more-data claim with: “AI learns patterns from examples. Useful, correctly labeled examples can help; poor examples can lead to mistakes.” Panel 14 motivates the bounded agent route. Stars, coins, 3/6 or 3/12 counts and extra locks do not add scope; there are three levels and badges.

Image 3 — gameplay/context only; user supplied, not visual design. Adapt records, links, ordering, tamper checks, quizzes and multiple copies into Chainy's style. Its flat cartoon look, BlockForge title, money, certificate, large network and extra levels are not requirements. “All data must match” needs a stated comparison rule; hashes reveal mismatches against references, not real-world truth.

Creative restrictions​

Keep the selected cute appearance with respectful English, practical scenarios and reasoning for ages 13–17. Avoid baby talk, long exposition and disruptive rewards. Proposed reusable production kit: one compact exploration scene with themed areas, one avatar, one guide rig with variants, shared blocks/nodes and modular scenery.

Proposed caps: 12 placeable objects per workbench, three replicas, two classifier classes, one short route. Cap overflow explains itself. Free construction must not become a prescribed spatial solution. Reduce scenery/effects/question variants before required concepts.

Recommended pre-final exclusions: real cryptocurrency/wallets, public blockchain, multiplayer, cloud AI, microphone, automatic cloud sync, teacher website, downloader, currency economy and certificates. No source caption adds authorization for extra scope. Use text/icons alongside color/audio; instructions work muted. Readability and touch/performance need real-device checks.

Game Design Definitions​

RecoveryPreserve data on migration/schema failure; UI-12 recovery. Switch ends active session and clears prior learner state before another starts.

M-02 — Map and progression (CH-R08; UI-03)​

ReferenceContract
O-02Two-world map with three level cards
P-02Locked/available/in-progress/completed; prerequisite; committed badge/checkpoint
A-02Inspect; start; continue; replay
RulesL1 initially available; saved L1 opens L2; saved L2 opens L3. Locked cards explain prerequisites. Replay starts a distinct session and preserves first attempts. All three complete → journey summary; map remains usable.

M-03 — Exploration and interaction (CH-R01, CH-R08; UI-04)​

ReferenceContract
O-03Explorer, guide, collectible context item, station
P-03Position; objective; collected item IDs; active target
A-03Move; approach; interact; read dialogue; collect; open station; pause
RulesNearest eligible highlighted target receives context action. Collect stable item once; repeated taps cannot duplicate it. Required items need no precision jumping/combat.
Feedback/recoveryQuest text/count changes; checkpoint restores collected IDs/objective. Missing/unreachable required item is a blocking defect, not learner failure.

Proposed tutorial: approach guide, Interact, collect/read one record, collect two remaining records, open station. Tracker says “Find delivery records: 1/3.” Guide reminders orient without revealing the answer.

M-04 — Free construction (CH-R02; UI-05)​

ReferenceContract
O-04Workbench, palette, blocks/nodes, typed ports
P-04Component ID/type; bounded position; payload/configuration; directed logical edges; undo state; cap
A-04Choose/preview/place; select/move/configure; connect/disconnect; remove; undo; test; reset
RulesCoordinates affect visibility/overlap only. Validation uses data/edges. Reject bounds/overlap/cap errors without changing the prior edit. Reject incompatible ports and explain missing links/branches/cycles under the activity rule.
RecoveryRemove removes incident links; undo restores prior edit/links. Reset confirms and restores scenario, never profile history. Test is a meaningful attempt; every touch is not evidence.

Choose palette part → tap location → Place; tap part → inspect/edit; Connect → source port → destination port. Drag is optional. Horizontal, L-shaped and other readable arrangements pass the same logical rules. Preview plus Confirm/Cancel prevents accidental changes.

M-05 — Records and ordering (CH-R03, CH-R08; UI-05)​

ReferenceContract
O-05Versioned delivery records in linked blocks
P-05ID, event/payload, predecessor reference, computed digest, ordering constraints
A-05Inspect; assign; link; compute/verify; revise
ValidatorExactly three required records once each; one connected acyclic sequence, one start/end; PACK before DISPATCH before RECEIVE; predecessor reference equals previous block's full digest. First uses declared genesis reference.
OutcomesValid; missing/duplicate record; wrong order; missing/broken predecessor; invalid topology. Highlight violated rule; remain editable.

Synthetic CH-L1-ORDER-01: P01 packs parcel K7 at A; D01 dispatches K7 from A to B; R01 receives K7 at B. Correct logical order P01 → D01 → R01. Scenario times 09:00/09:10/09:20 may be displayed; actual device clocks never decide correctness. P01 → R01 → D01 fails because receive needs prior dispatch. P01 placed on the right remains valid with correct logical links.

Canonical serialization and full digests govern checks; shortened display values never do. Freeze field order/encoding/hash contract with the technical owner before agents implement it. One scaffold demonstrates a link, then learners create an independent arrangement.

M-06 — Tamper detection and integrity (CH-R04; UI-06)​

ReferenceContract
O-06Working ledger and immutable trusted scenario checkpoint
P-06Current payload/digest; predecessor references; trusted version; comparison
A-06Inspect; introduce test alteration; verify; compare; restore; retest
ValidatorRecompute working digest, inspect successor references and compare with trusted scenario checkpoint. Explain internal link consistency and reference agreement separately.
OutcomesUnchanged; data mismatch; broken link; missing trusted reference. Missing reference cannot establish historical correctness.

Synthetic CH-L2-TAMPER-01: change D01 destination B to C. Verification detects a changed digest and R01's stale link. Recompute every following hash: links can now be internally consistent, but the chain still differs from the trusted B destination. Learner identifies the change, restores the supplied trusted value and verifies. Say “This differs from our saved exercise reference,” not “A hash proves this delivery happened.” Later blocks remain visible; change does not erase stored blocks. A maliciously replaced trusted reference is outside the exercise's assurance.

M-07 — Three-replica synchronization (CH-R05; UI-06)​

ReferenceContract
O-07Replica nodes A, B, C on one device
P-07Ledger per node; internal validity; comparison signature; connection graph; repair candidate
A-07Freely place/connect; inspect; introduce mismatch; compare; preview repair; confirm
RuleStated normal exercise assumption: at most one corrupted copy. Two identical internally valid copies propose a candidate. One differing copy is repaired only after preview/confirmation. All three nodes must form one connected inspection graph; line and triangle arrangements both work, while self-links are rejected. All-different, insufficient valid matches or missing required connections → no automatic repair.
FeedbackAgreement/disagreement per node; candidate source and exact changed fields. Label as simplified classroom rule, not secure real-world consensus.

Synthetic CH-L2-REPLICAS-01: A/B contain P01,D01,R01 with B destination; C contains destination C. A+B propose B under the one-corrupt-copy assumption. A separate stress case deliberately breaks that assumption: three different versions show “No agreement—inspect the trusted checkpoint.” It teaches the boundary of the classroom rule, not a case the one-corrupt-copy promise can resolve. An explicitly selected provided checkpoint resolves that case. Completion requires connected replicas matching the scenario reference after explicit reconciliation and a learner explanation; arbitrary shared corruption is insufficient. No mining/network transport or proof of distributed security is implied.

M-08 — Example-trained classification (CH-R06; UI-07)​

ReferenceContract
O-08Training examples, two class prototypes, held-out test parcels
P-08Normalized numeric feature vector; learner label; training/test membership; hidden content truth; fitted version; squared distances
A-08Inspect; label; train; predict unseen item; reveal/evaluate; revise; retrain
RuleProposed local C# nearest-centroid model: mean vector per class, smaller squared Euclidean distance predicts class. At least one valid example per required class; test items excluded from fit. Out-of-range/invalid features reject fitting. Distance difference within proposed 0.000001 tolerance → Ambiguous.
OutcomesNot trained; insufficient classes/examples; invalid features; prediction; ambiguous tie. Edits make model stale until retrained. Show distances, never an invented probability.

Synthetic CH-L3-CLASSIFY-01: fictional parcels have weight and fragility, each a scenario feature normalized to 0–1, not sensor/image input. Standard examples (0.2,0.2), (0.4,0.2) give centroid (0.3,0.2); Careful examples (0.6,0.8), (0.8,0.8) give (0.7,0.8). Held-out T1=(0.6,0.6), content truth Careful, has squared distances 0.25 and 0.05 and predicts Careful. T2=(0.5,0.5) has 0.13 to both and returns Ambiguous.

Designed error: relabel (0.6,0.8) Standard and retrain. Standard becomes (0.4,0.4), Careful (0.8,0.8); T1 ties at 0.08. Another held-out parcel, T3=(0.55,0.55), has content truth Careful. The clean model's distances are 0.185 and 0.085, predicting Careful; the mislabeled model's distances become 0.045 and 0.125, incorrectly predicting Standard. Correcting the label restores Careful. This demonstrates both ambiguity and an actual wrong prediction caused by changed examples, without claiming more examples always improve a model. Content truth is a synthetic scenario label, not independently established real-world truth. Hide it before first prediction. Completion requires training/testing, inspecting the designed error/tie, revision/retraining and recorded comparison; repeated clicks without changed examples are insufficient. Quiz assesses interpretation, not perfect accuracy.

Pending theme choice: parcels remain the existing default. Image 2's cats/dogs may replace the theme after choosing teachable explicit features and reviewed data. That must not silently turn the activity into computer vision or hard-coded sorting. Theme affects final content/art; example-trained behavior stays required.

M-09 — Observe–decide–act delivery agent (CH-R07; UI-08)​

ReferenceContract
O-09Agent, bounded grid, parcel, destination, obstacle
P-09Cell/orientation; carrying; visible ordered policy; observation; command index; steps/trace
A-09Add/reorder/remove Move, Turn Left/Right, Pick Up, Deliver, Stop; optional one If obstacle ahead then Turn Right; step/run/stop/reset
RuleProposed 12-command/20-executed-step caps. Each dispatched command consumes one step. An optional If command turns right when the next cell is blocked, otherwise leaves position/facing unchanged; either case advances to the next command, with no implicit loop. Actions update deterministically. Blocked/out-of-grid Move stops; Pick Up requires parcel here and empty hands; Deliver requires carrying at destination. Exhausting commands without delivery is stopped incomplete.
OutcomesRunning; delivered; blocked; invalid action; stopped incomplete; step limit. Retain trace and editable policy.

Synthetic CH-L3-AGENT-01: 4×3 grid, x=0–3 eastward and y=0–2 northward. Start (0,0) facing east, parcel (1,0), obstacle (2,0), destination (1,2). Success: Move, Pick Up, Turn Left, Move, Move, Deliver. Move again after pickup hits obstacle and stops at (1,0), still carrying. Trace shows observation → action → state, motivating the turn. Reset restores agent/parcel while retaining policy; separate Clear Policy confirms deletion. Delivered is set only by the carrying/destination rule. This is visible rule-based behavior, not generative AI or reinforcement learning.

M-10 — Formative quiz (CH-R08; UI-09)​

ReferenceContract
O-10Versioned question set and submitted answer
P-10Objective/item ID; first answer; current attempt; correctness; hints/retries; gate
A-10Read; select; submit; inspect explanation; retry
RuleProposed three distinct questions per level; all three need a saved response and at least two distinct questions must be correct under their latest saved answers, plus all required activities complete. Retrying one item replaces only its current gate result and never counts the same item twice; original answers remain immutable evidence. This is a tuning default, not a school pass mark or validated research instrument. Disable repeat submit while saving. A deliberate new answer is a new attempt.
FeedbackExplain correct and wrong choices. Preserve first-attempt evidence and assistance separately from eventual completion.

Review items: L1—why receive follows dispatch, meaning of predecessor, whether spatial movement changes validity. L2—detecting altered-and-rehashed records, all-different copies, hashes versus truth. L3—incorrect labels, tied distances, observation justifying an agent turn. Educator review supplies final distractors/copy. Research pre/post instruments are separately versioned.

M-11 — Persistence, checkpoints and rewards (CH-R08, CH-R09; UI-10/11)​

ReferenceContract
O-11SQLite attempt, session, progress checkpoint, level badge
P-11Stable event/session IDs; immutable build/content version; first/retry result; checkpoint; completion
A-11Commit; retry same save; resume; replay; show summary
RuleAttempt/checkpoint/completion commit together. Only successful commit displays badge/unlock. Failed-save retry reuses event ID; deliberate gameplay retry uses a new one. One badge per learner/level; later attempts stay separate.
RecoveryBusy/full/failed save explains retry; no false success or silent deletion. Restart after process loss marks unfinished session interrupted and resumes in a new session.

Checkpoint defaults: mission start after session creation; collected-object milestones; activity entry; successful activity completion; committed quiz result; level completion. Store logical state, not animations. Uncommitted edits may be lost; exit/resume copy explains this. Android pause requests a safe checkpoint but is not the sole save trigger. Incompatible/missing checkpoints explain safe restart while retaining historical evidence. Failed answers remain evidence and never become retrospectively correct.

M-12 — Staff export and recovery (CH-R10, CH-R11; UI-12)​

ReferenceContract
O-12Selected study/session scope, export, receipt
P-12Count; schema/build/content versions; export ID; integrity digest; written/receipt states
A-12Preview; export through Android picker; cancel/retry; transfer externally; inspect importer counts; record receipt
RuleConsistent logical snapshot with approved pseudonymous fields. Cancel/failure preserves records. File saved differs from transferred/received. Re-import deduplicates stable IDs; malformed/conflicting records rejected/quarantined.
RecoveryRetry without delete-on-export. Signed in-place update must retain tested records. Code cannot recover uninstalled data.

Identity maps, participant exports, credentials and signing keys stay out of repositories/issues. Teacher-held identity mapping makes evidence pseudonymous. Staff navigation/optional convenience PIN is not strong authentication. Custody, transfer and retention need the approved offline evidence plan.

Layout and control mapping​

Proposed landscape HUD: objective top-left, Pause top-right, stick lower-left, context button lower-right; center stays clear. Workbench replaces movement with palette/selection. Test and Back are separated from Reset; selected-object panels must not cover ports. Dialogue has speaker, short text and explicit advance.
